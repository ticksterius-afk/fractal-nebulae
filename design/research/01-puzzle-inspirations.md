# Puzzle-game inspirations for Fractal Nebulae

Research report, 2026-10-02. Scope: what existing puzzle games can teach us about turning a relaxing 6-DoF fractal flight app (raymarched Mandelbulb / Mandelbox / Menger / Julia / Sierpinski / Apollonian / Kleinian / IFS / Lichtenberg nebulae, two lensing black holes, resonance pulse, gravity glide, target-lock codex, generative music, ghost mode, infinite zoom) into a puzzle game. Every claim below is tied to a fetched article, talk page, review or aggregated-review source; the full list is in section (e). Where a source could not be reached (several Thinky Games and Rock Paper Shotgun pages return 403) I say so rather than quote from memory.

---

## (a) Per-game notes

### 1. Exploration-puzzle and spatial-reasoning games

**Outer Wilds (Mobius Digital, 2019).** Core verb: *learn*. Progression is purely knowledge-based; the ship log stores everything you discover "either by location or in a web of connections" and is the only thing that persists across the 22-minute loop. The developers describe the structure as "a set of smaller mysteries, which each encourage exploration to solve and reward the player with new knowledge" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds)). Alex Beachum: "We want to remove aimless wandering from the design ... we give you enough information that you make conscious choices," using visible destinations and trailmarkers rather than objectives ([Mobius blog](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/the-intentionality-of-wandering)). GDC talks cover curiosity-driven design and "4D level design" where levels pace information inside a physics sim that changes over time ([GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/see-4d-level-design-outer-wilds-deconstructed-gdc-2020), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027008/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity)). Aggregated Steam complaints: frustration/feeling stuck (22% of negative weight), ship and suit controls (20%), convoluted puzzles with thin clues (15%), repetitive loop (13%), lack of guidance (11%) ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/753640/outer_wilds.md)). Critics specifically disliked dying or running out of loop "due to what they perceived as unfairly difficult or unexpected gameplay." Lesson: knowledge-gating works, but a 6-DoF flight model plus timers is the exact combination players found punishing.

**The Witness (Thekla, 2016).** Core verb: *draw a line that satisfies hidden rules*. About 650 panel puzzles plus optional environmental puzzles "where a single path is disguised in the environment" and found by moving to the right viewpoint; Blow estimated ~80 hours for everything. "Rules are taught to the player throughout the course of the game by the puzzles themselves"; one critic praised that "impossible, incomprehensible puzzles melt into simple exercises after you've visited nearby locations," while others said there is "simply too much going on to give proper feedback about what I'm getting wrong" and warned the lack of guidance could make players "bounce off entirely" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witness_(2016_video_game))). Blow's stated aim: "simulating an experience of epiphany ... something kind of important," with strong colours and little texture so solutions register visually ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/shared-epiphany-jonathan-blow-reflects-on-the-witness)). Narrow FOV caused motion sickness until patched; relevant for a raymarched flight game.

**Manifold Garden (William Chyr, 2019).** Core verb: *change which wall is the floor*; six gravity directions, each colour-coded, in a world that repeats infinitely so falling off a structure lands you on its next copy. Chyr kept every outdoor level visually distinct and used windows as orientation anchors; about 2,000 hours of playtesting went into navigability ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_Garden)). He deleted tutorial-style "exampuzzles" as "really tedious": "every single puzzle highlights something interesting, and that's it." Players found jumping into the void "very daunting," so the first puzzle forces that leap ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-manifold-garden-i-makes-reality-fold-back-on-itself)). All surfaces had to be 90-degree because the mechanic only works on six planes ([Automaton interview](https://automaton-media.com/en/playism/20210325-598/)). Directly relevant: self-repeating space and disorientation are our default state.

**Antichamber (Alexander Bruce, 2013).** Core verb: *discard assumptions about space*. Teaching tools: a hub map that "fill[s] in as the player visits specific rooms, highlighting passages the player has yet to explore," pictographic sign plaques after each puzzle, and guns that each add a verb (store cubes, grow, chain, fill) while keeping earlier ones. Metacritic 82; Game Informer said players "rarely ever felt stuck or frustrated"; 100,000 copies in two months, mostly week one ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antichamber), [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/antichamber-sales)). Bruce: "breaking down all those expectations and then remaking them is essentially the core mechanic."

**Superliminal (Pillow Castle, 2019).** Core verb: *resize by perspective*. Critics agree the trick "immediately makes sense" and escalates creatively, but "it's a less versatile mechanic" than portals, puzzles rate "3/10 on difficulty," the back half becomes a walking sim, length is 2 to 6 hours and many Steam reviews say wait for a sale ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/superliminal/superliminal-review-a-matter-of-perspective), [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1049410/superliminal), [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/corey-motley/superliminal-review-2)). One defence: "its priorities are discovery and sense pleasure." That is a useful framing for a relaxing fractal game, but the complaint about shallow puzzles is the main risk.

**Viewfinder (Sad Owl, 2023).** Core verb: *place a photograph into the world as solid geometry*. Universally praised concept; puzzles rated "mixed" because "players can brute-force their way through challenges by snapping multiple photos" and "whole chunks of chapters pass with minimal friction" (61% at [Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/reviews/viewfinder-review-picture-perfect-concept-mixed-puzzles/)). Pattern to avoid: a mechanic so free that elegance is optional.

**Monument Valley (ustwo, 2014).** Core verb: *rotate the structure until impossible paths connect*. Ken Wong's GDC Europe talk "Less Game, More Experience": the team chose "to trade challenging puzzles for an aesthetic journey," kept it "short but sublime," and found in testing that players stare at the protagonist so environmental actions must be visually loud ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021380/Designing-Monument-Valley-Less), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/gdc-europe-2014-adds-standout-talks-by-i-monument-valley-i-i-luftrausers-i-devs)). No failure states, no stars, no grind. Closest tonal match to Fractal Nebulae.

**Cocoon (Geometric Interactive, 2023).** Core verb: *carry a world inside a world*. Jeppe Carlsen started from "What if the different levels of a game were also objects which you could pick up?" and found he "constantly needed to 'zoom out' and consider the bigger picture"; he cut a rhythm orb and stealth because they distracted; "It's never fun to get really stuck in a linear puzzle game, but in COCOON, I felt that it got even more frustrating" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-challenges-of-laying-worlds-upon-worlds-in-puzzle-game-cocoon)). One stick and one button. Steam reviews: puzzle design praised near-universally, but 65% of negative weight is "repetitive and tedious actions, such as moving orbs back and forth," 46% dislike the one-hit boss fights, 34% cite 3 to 7 hours for the price ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1497440/cocoon.md)).

**Tunic (Andrew Shouldice, 2022).** Core verb: *read a manual you cannot read*. Pages in a constructed (not cipher) language carry "drawings, maps, diagrams, and handwritten notes" that teach mechanics and hide the Golden Path ending; some critics felt "obscure puzzles could leave players stuck, particularly near the end" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic_(video_game))). Shouldice's GDC 2023 model: secrets as "loose ends," and "the joy of resolving them is not always better than the joy of having lots of tantalizing mysteries" ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/unlock-secrets-tunic-gdc-2023-design-session)). Our codex cards are an obvious manual analogue.

**Chants of Sennaar (Rundisc, 2023).** Core verb: *deduce glyph meaning from context, then validate*. A notebook lets you match glyphs to drawings; "if they are all correct on the page, the correct translations will be given." Puzzles escalate into interpreting between factions; criticisms are that some puzzles are "a bit too convoluted" and navigation is disorienting ([TheSixthAxis](https://thesixthaxis.com/2023/09/26/chants-of-sennaar-review), [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/chants-of-sennaar)). The languages "are puzzle elements like any others," designed for play, not linguistics ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/31644-chants-of-sennaar-devs-explain-how-the-game-s-fictional-languages-were-developed)). The batch-validation loop is a clean, text-free hint system.

**Blue Prince (Dogubomb, 2025).** Core verb: *draft rooms, carry knowledge between runs*. OpenCritic 99th percentile; praised as "puzzle design rethought for a roguelike loop," criticised for "excessive RNG required for certain puzzle solutions, especially in the mid to late game" ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/18352/blue-prince/reviews?page=5)). AV Club's two critics split: randomness "forces tactical thinking" versus "intensely dispiriting" walls and a post-game that was "a bridge too far" ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/blue-prince-game-debate)). Lesson: seeded variation is fine; RNG-gated solutions are not.

**Animal Well (Billy Basso, 2024).** Core verb: *use a small kit of multi-purpose tools to uncover layered secrets*. Three explicit layers: a complete game for everyone, hidden discoveries, and community puzzles "not exactly intended to be easily solvable by one person" (a 50-tile image solved on Discord). Basso: "No matter what you put in a game, it will get found"; museums "designed to be viewed in any order" inspired level design; standard double-jump/dash abilities were avoided ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/features/interview-how-animal-well-is-using-secrets-and-mysteries-to-be-a-different-kind-of-metroidvania/), [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/best-of-2024-i-avoided-almost-all-the-existing-tropes-peeling-back-the-layers-of-animal-well)).

**Lorelei and the Laser Eyes (Simogo, 2024).** Core verb: *observe, take notes, open locks*. A "notebook puzzler" with many parallel routes so "you're unlikely to hit a complete roadblock," but its in-fiction hint phone gives "an obtuse phrase from an untrustworthy character," which reviewers called "cruel" and said made players "feel stupid," on top of a menu with no back button ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/lorelei-and-the-laser-eyes-review-a-notebook-puzzler-with-spooky-vibes/), [CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/lorelei-and-the-laser-eyes-review)). Anti-pattern: a hint channel that is itself a puzzle.

**The Talos Principle 2 (Croteam, 2023).** Core verbs: *route lasers through connectors, press, carry, swap*. Twelve regions, each introduces one mechanic then retires it; 8 of 10 puzzles per region are mandatory; 132 puzzles, ~20 hours; mines, turrets and timers from the first game were removed so difficulty is "entirely" cerebral; the hardest content is environmental (Prometheus hide-and-seek, Sphinx ciphers, Pandora connect-the-dots) ([EIP](https://eip.gg/reviews/the-talos-principle-2-review)). Some veterans found it "a severely watered down version" of TP1 and said difficulty "never ramped up" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/835960/discussions/0/3950280599879618385)). The connector rule set (one connector feeds every target it can see; beams cannot cross) is a good template for a beam puzzle ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/835960/discussions/0/6364229920743795207)).

**Islands of Insight (Lunarch, 2024).** 10,000+ puzzles in a shared open world; OpenCritic 71st percentile. Praised for breadth, calm, a generous logic-hint system and the freedom to skip; criticised because "individual puzzles lack depth," the first five hours feel basic, platforming is awkward, and an always-online requirement lost one reviewer's progress ([Room Escape Artist](https://roomescapeartist.com/2024/05/08/islands-insight-review/), [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/16292/-/reviews)). A cautionary tale about quantity.

**Hyperbolica (CodeParade, 2022).** Steam 84% positive of 1,223. Not a puzzle game so much as a tour: shooting in spherical space, drone racing and a labyrinth in hyperbolic space, skating, platforming. The store page's thesis: "first-hand experience really is the best way to understand and build an intuition about these interesting geometries" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1256230/Hyperbolica/)). One reviewer praised the choice "to make a math-based adventure game rather than puzzles" because curiosity alone taught the geometry ([GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/hyperbolica)). This is the strongest argument that Fractal Nebulae's puzzles should showcase the math rather than stump.

**4D Golf (CodeParade, 2024).** Overwhelmingly Positive; 120+ levels, a level editor, and "tons of visualization options" (spin around the 4th axis, ghost images of what is perpendicular to you). "You don't have to bother with the complicated definition of 4D, as the game will soon let you know what it is and let you feel it via the unique gameplay" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/get-the-hang-of-4d-easily-in-this-unique-casual-golfing-game)). Golf is the familiar container for an unfamiliar space; our familiar container is flying.

**Portal (Valve, 2007).** Core verb: *place two linked holes*. Lessons from the GDC postmortems: weekly playtests from week one; when the energy-ball puzzle failed, "we broke this concept out into another two sections which involved redirecting an energy ball only once"; a confusing force field became glass; "gates" (mandatory pauses with particles and sound) make players notice the portal gun; puzzles were built to break wrong assumptions (that orange portals are exits only); Wolpaw's rule to reduce "the delta between story and gameplay" ([Game Developer: Thinking With Portals](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/thinking-with-portals-creating-valve-s-new-ip), [Game Developer: Secrets of Portal](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/best-of-gdc-the-secrets-of-i-portal-i-s-huge-success), [Portal wiki commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary)). Length around 3 hours.

### 2. Mechanics that fit a flight / zoom / scale context

**Scale-based.** Superliminal (above). *Scale* (Steve Swink) was a "shrink-and-grow ray" that "suck[s] the size out of one object and blast[s] it into other objects," which drifted from puzzles toward exploration as the gun became overpowered ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-09-18-the-union-of-scale-gravity-ghost-and-the-indie-devs-making-them.html)). *Maquette* nests the world inside a model of itself; Eurogamer liked that "there's more emphasis on step-by-step logic here, and less on tricks of perception," but reviewers hit "a frustrating amount of back-and-forth between structures" and "only one rigid solution" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquette_(video_game)), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/maquette-review-exquisite-puzzler-that-doesnt-quite-fit-together-2898296)). Takeaway: scale puzzles need a logic layer, not just a perception trick, and traversal between scales must be fast.

**Light / beam / reflection.** Talos connectors (above). Rule sets that work: line of sight, one emitter feeding several receivers, beams that must not cross, colours that must not mix.

**Orbit / gravity.** *Gravity Ghost* turns "the slingshot-around-Jupiter scenario ... into a casual, colorful maneuver that inspires fun and experimentation instead of a strict fealty to reality"; a reviewer called it "a relaxing exercise in orbital physics" and "an exercise in give and take" ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/gravity-ghost-review-stars-and-science-forever), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_Ghost)); Erin Robinson's postmortem stresses abandoning ideas and "learning what to listen for when playtesting" ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022023/contactUs)). *Orbit - Playing with Gravity* (HIGHKEY): flick planets into stable orbits around black holes, with the future trajectory drawn before launch; 45 levels; Google Play Indie Festival winner ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id991457817)). Kerbal Space Program: see section 3.

**Sound / music.** *Chants of Sennaar* (above) is really a decoding puzzle. *Sound Shapes*: coins correspond to instruments; "every action you take creates music"; levels began "with a song, in other cases a style"; community levels died when servers shut in 2018 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Shapes), [Game Revolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/previews/52325-sound-shapes-preview)). *Rez*: sound effects are replaced by musical cues the player triggers, "you may be affecting or creating part of the musicality" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rez_(video_game)), [FACT](https://factmag.com/2017/01/10/rez-infinite-tetsuya-mizuguchi-interview)). These are feedback systems more than puzzle systems; our generative music can be both.

**Self-similarity / recursion.** *Patrick's Parabox*: boxes contain levels that contain boxes; escalation runs enter-box, nested boxes, "boxes that are actually the level itself," then pushing a box out into "the void" for infinity symbols ([SuperJump](https://superjumpmagazine.com/draft-patricks-parabox-recursive-puzzles)). Traynor's GDC 2024 slides: "The purpose of the puzzles is to showcase this cool system"; "Approachable difficulty. To showcase/communicate, not challenge/stump"; "Find an interaction and force it"; look for "'Things almost happen' moments" and build "'Show' levels"; enumerate mechanic pairs; 364 shipped puzzles from 600+ drafts; ~15 full-game narrated video playtests with no diminishing returns; smoothing tools are "Insert, modify, delete, reorder, optionalize" ([GDC slides PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Traynor_Patrick_SystemCentricPuzzle.pdf)). Early builds had too few intro puzzles and a curve that was "way too steep" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-patrick-traynor-s-i-patrick-s-parabox-i-)). *Recursed*: chests contain rooms, rooms reset, one item carried; one solution per puzzle, no hints, uneven curve, but worlds can be skipped; it is literally RE-complete ([Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/analysis/recursed), [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.05131)). *Gorogoa*: zooming into and out of panels until "the border between them dissolves"; Roberts wanted solutions "to feel miraculous" and refused objects that exist "only in order to connect with something else" ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/28053-gorogoa-developer-jason-roberts-interview)).

**Fractal mazes.** Mark J. P. Wolf's Fractal Maze (2003): a maze containing lettered copies of itself; you record each copy you enter and must exit "in reverse order of entry, like a series of nested boxes"; the twist is that "wrong-way paths [are] of infinite length," so neither dead ends nor loops signal failure; three solutions of increasing depth exist ([MathPuzzle](https://www.mathpuzzle.com/18Nov2003.html)). This is the single most direct template for a Menger/Sierpinski navigation puzzle, and nobody has shipped it in 3D.

### 3. Educational / science puzzle games that are good games

**Zachtronics (Opus Magnum etc.).** Zach Barth: "as long as the toolset is expressive and the problem is open-ended, we will see creative solutions from players"; leaderboards drive optimisation without in-game rewards because optimisers "are often more intrinsically motivated"; "it's satisfying to watch factories work, and especially so when it's one of your own design"; GIF export lets players share solutions ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-zachtronics-i-opus-magnum-i-)).

**Kerbal Space Program.** A 2025 teaching paper used KSP to let students discover Kepler's laws: orbits visibly turn elliptical under impulses, readouts show speed rising at periapsis, and periods are compared across altitudes; students show "a deep understanding of their orbital maneuvers" after a Moon-landing capstone. Features credited: accurate physics, real-time visual feedback, low-stakes repeated failure, on-screen parameters ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13259v1)). Another writer: "after a few days playing KSP, it comes naturally. No mathematical calculations or equations are necessary" ([Fourmilab](https://fourmilab.ch/images/kerbal/)).

**Foldit.** Motivation studies found "contributing to scientific research and an interest in science" started people, while community and "the intellectual challenge of the game" kept them; the team ran the game "like an ongoing, continuous experiment" with updates every couple of weeks ([Open University study](https://oro.open.ac.uk/44708), [American Scientist](https://www.americanscientist.org/blog/from-the-staff/behind-the-scenes-of-foldit-pioneering-science-gamification)).

**Human Resource Machine.** "The learning curve is well-crafted ... gamers are never underprepared," with optional optimisation challenges; the recurring criticism is that it is "dry for all but maths enthusiasts" ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/wii-u/human-resource-machine-2), [Bonus Stage](https://www.bonusstage.co.uk/archives/57306)).

**Turing Complete.** NAND gates to a working CPU; 96% positive on Steam; one reviewer condensed "weeks" of a college course into "a couple hours of game time" ([Embedded Artistry](https://embeddedartistry.com/blog/2021/11/29/learning-computer-architecture-through-a-game/), [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1444480/turing_complete.md)); an ASEE paper reports using it in a digital-logic course ([ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/45615)).

**Baba Is You.** Rules are pushable blocks. Teikari designs by "thinking of cool or amusing combinations/interactions between the words, and then working backwards to make up a level that requires using said interactions"; "the most satisfying moments in puzzle games are those which present the player with simple but hard-to-wrap-your-head-around situations" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-hempuli-oy-s-i-baba-is-you-i-)). He has said difficulty is not the goal; the level's "trick" must feel satisfying or devious ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/)). A planned hint system was shelved for time ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/736260/discussions/0/1639793203770588137)).

**Why learning feels like play in these:** the simulation is honest (KSP, Turing Complete), feedback is immediate and visual, failure is cheap, the toolset is small but expressive, optimisation is optional and social (Zachtronics), and the thing being learned *is* the mechanic rather than a quiz bolted onto a mechanic. Eco was not researched in this pass.

### 4. Design principles

**Blow and ten Bosch, "Designing to Reveal the Nature of the Universe" (IndieCade 2011).** "Any system of interactivity can of course be explored: If X happens, what are the consequences?" The job is to "find the core ideas of the system, and see how those ideas illustrate fundamental truths of our universe," as opposed to "more-traditional combinatoric design techniques" ([Marc ten Bosch](https://marctenbosch.com/news/?p=181)). A report from the talk adds two rules: puzzles "need not be difficult," and you must eliminate random solution paths so players cannot solve by accident ([IndieGameReviewer](https://indiegamereviewer.com/?p=6698)).

**Elyot Grant, "30 Puzzle Design Lessons" (GDC / Extended Director's Cut, 2021).** Draws on escape rooms, puzzlehunts, crosswords, logic puzzles and chess compositions ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027306/30-Puzzle-Design-Lessons-From)). Central definition: a puzzle is "anything that conceals a Eureka Moment," the "sudden, pleasureful, fluent, confident feeling of understanding"; Eureka and Fiero (pride at overcoming difficulty) are orthogonal, Eureka is shareable and, per Grant, cannot be spoiled, and difficulty-for-its-own-sake buys only Fiero ([Diplograph](https://diplograph.net/notes/games/puzzle-design/eureka-is-not-fiero), [Electron Dance](https://www.electrondance.com/soar/)). Taiji's designer recommends it as essential reading and describes "forwards design" from deducible situations plus "happy accidents," and the rule that if most players dislike a puzzle "the presentation likely needs improvement" ([Taiji blog](https://taiji-game.com/2022/02/23/84-how-i-design-puzzles/)). Electron Dance's critique of Taiji is instructive: puzzles that mix visual decryption with logic produced "relief rather than euphoria."

**Catch and revelation (GMTK, as summarised).** A good puzzle has a clear goal, a "catch" (two things in apparent conflict), and a "revelation" that often subverts an assumption; show only what is needed; sequence so later puzzles combine earlier concepts. Brett Taylor's "noise" rule: anything that consumes memory without contributing to the solution is noise. "Describe the solution in a sentence," then build the smallest puzzle around it ([Hamatti notes](https://notes.hamatti.org/gaming/puzzle-game-design), [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-puzzle-games-difficulty-curve)).

**Thinky / honest puzzles (Hazelden).** Puzzles exist "not to increase playtime, nor to hide a deficiency ... but rather to communicate interesting ideas" ([CESCG](https://cescg.org/?p=5943)). In A Monster's Expedition "the player can do everything in the game at the beginning, even if they don't know it yet," and "if you're stuck on one puzzle, there's usually another elsewhere" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-relaxing-open-world-puzzle-design-of-i-a-monster-s-expedition-i-)).

**Hints and stuck players.** Recommended pattern: optional, escalating, non-spoiling hints ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-hint-system-for-puzzles)). A St Andrews thesis found that hints which "guide players towards easier deductions rather than ... completing the next cell for them" were as effective, felt less like cheating, and were used far more ([St Andrews](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/effective-player-guidance-in-logic-puzzles/)). A University of Washington study of adaptive hints reported players prefer "concise, accurate, implicit" hints and that abstract hints are "worse than no hint" ([UW paper](https://faculty.washington.edu/weicaics/paper/papers/HaoHZC2022.pdf)). Structural alternatives to hints: Outer Wilds' rumor web, Antichamber's auto-filling map, Talos 2's 8-of-10 rule, Islands' skip-anything, Chants' notebook validation, Monster's Expedition's parallel islands.

**Count and length norms (from the sources above).** Portal ~3 h; Superliminal 2 to 6 h; Cocoon 3 to 7 h; Talos 1 ~10 h; Talos 2 132 puzzles / ~20 h; Railbound 240+ puzzles / 5 to 10 h ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1304610/discussions/0/614286717396989541)); Parabox 364 puzzles; The Witness ~650 puzzles / ~80 h; Islands of Insight 10,000 / ~30 h campaign; Monument Valley deliberately "short but sublime." For a free browser game the relevant norm is Wordle's: one puzzle a day, a few minutes, a spoiler-free share grid.

**Difficulty curve.** Portal: isolate each concept into its own chamber, then recombine. Parabox: insert, modify, delete, reorder, optionalize; bite-sized; "how easy is too easy?" traded depth for approachability. Talos 2: introduce a mechanic per region, then retire it. Islands of Insight and Parabox both show the first hour is where curves fail (too flat or too steep).

### 5. Short browser / web puzzle games that found audiences

- **The Roottrees are Dead**: Global Game Jam 2023 prototype, free browser build on itch (Nov 2023, ~8,000 downloads, $3,000 in tips the first month), remade in Godot with a second chapter, Steam launch Jan 2025, over $1M revenue. "Free versions don't cannibalize sales"; "itch.io functions as an incubator" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4682)).
- **Die in the Dungeon**: jam game, 919k browser plays, "99% of the marketing was organic," 1,694 Steam reviews in three months. **Tiny Kingdom** is the counter-example: 141k browser plays and 10k wishlists, but only 107 reviews because the Steam build barely differed from the demo. Rule: itch success is "necessary but not sufficient"; look for 1,000+ daily view spikes and ship a visibly upgraded build ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/22/more-games-that-made-the-itch-io-to-steam-transition/)).
- Cookie Clicker, A Dark Room, Threes!, Helltaker: "idle, narrative, and polished puzzle titles where short sessions translate to long-tail play" perform best on Steam ([DinoGame](https://dinogame.gg/blog/browser-games-that-became-steam-games/)).
- Antichamber: 100k units in two months on Steam ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/antichamber-sales)).
- **Wordle**: 90 players on 1 Nov 2021, 300k by 2 Jan 2022, 2M weekly a week later, 4.8 billion plays in 2023; "one puzzle per day creates a sense of scarcity"; the emoji grid shares a result without spoiling it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). Thinky Games is building "Thinky Dailies," where the rules change every day ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/news/announcing-our-big-new-project-thinky-dailies-a-story-told-through-daily-puzzles-where-the-rules-change-every-day/)).

Implication for a GitHub Pages WebGL app: a short, seeded, shareable daily loop is the proven web format; a 2 to 4 hour authored campaign is the proven Steam format; the browser build is the incubator for the second.

## (b) Mechanic to Fractal Nebulae mapping

| Mechanic (source) | What it is | How it maps onto Fractal Nebulae |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge as the only key (Outer Wilds) | Progress persists as facts, not items | Codex cards are the ship log; a web of "rumors" (coordinates, scale, depth) replaces locks; nothing gates but knowing where and how deep to go |
| Viewpoint alignment (Witness environmental, Monument Valley, Viewfinder) | Fragments connect only from one vantage | Einstein-ring / multiple-image alignment around the black holes; Sierpinski or Kleinian silhouettes that resolve into a glyph from one position |
| Perspective scaling (Superliminal, Scale) | Size is a function of distance | Ship scale already tracks local structure; a gate only admits a ship "sized" by approaching through a region of the right iteration depth |
| Nested worlds and recursion (Parabox, Recursed, Cocoon, Maquette, Gorogoa) | Enter a copy of the world inside itself | Infinite zoom is literal recursion; a "depth stack" HUD records which sub-copy you are inside; exiting in reverse order (Wolf's fractal maze) is the puzzle |
| Fractal maze with infinite wrong ways (Wolf) | Copies within copies; no dead ends, only endless descent | Menger / Sierpinski beacon hunt where wrong turns go deeper forever; the hint is the depth counter |
| Gravity direction and world wrap (Manifold Garden) | Change what counts as down; fall onto the next copy | Gravity glide around a black hole changes "down"; Mandelbox fold symmetry means flying out one side re-enters another |
| Non-Euclidean connections (Antichamber, Hyperbolica) | Space does not obey your mental map | Wormholes as the graph edges; the auto-filling map (Antichamber) shows unexplored exits |
| Hidden dimension slicing (4D Golf, Miegakure) | Rotate to reveal perpendicular structure | The quaternion Julia set is a 3-D slice of 4-D; a "slice" control rotates the hidden axis and ghost-renders the perpendicular (4D Golf's ghost images) |
| Beam routing (Talos connectors) | Line-of-sight chains, non-crossing colours | Resonance pulse as an emitter; reflective facets of the Apollonian spheres as mirrors; one pulse must light N receivers |
| Orbital slingshot with predicted path (Orbit, Gravity Ghost, KSP) | Set a vector, read the trajectory, go | Gravity glide shows the predicted path; thread rings at periapsis; teach Kepler via a speed readout |
| Time as a resource (Outer Wilds loop, Blue Prince runs) | Things change on a clock | Time dilation near the horizon: slow a pattern down to read it, or wait "outside" for a distant clock |
| Decoding a language in context (Chants, Tunic) | Glyphs validated in batches | Codex glyphs for iterate / fold / scale; validation page like Chants' notebook; wormhole addresses written in them |
| Sound as feedback and as clue (Rez, Sound Shapes) | Actions make music | Each fractal's music already echoes its math; a missing motif is found by flying to the depth where it plays |
| Rule manipulation (Baba Is You) | The rules are objects | Expose one fractal parameter (power, fold, c) as a dial the player sets to open a route |
| Expressive toolset, optional optimisation (Zachtronics) | Many solutions, leaderboards, shareable replays | Route-finding with length / pulses used as scores; share a seed string instead of a server (Sound Shapes' lesson) |
| Layered secrets (Animal Well, Tunic) | Layer 1 for all, 2 for the curious, 3 for the community | Layer 1 = daily puzzle; 2 = codex-hidden coordinates; 3 = a community-only constant encoded across nebulae |
| Teaching by gating attention (Portal gates) | Force a pause so the player sees the tool | The first resonance pulse fires automatically with a sound and bloom; the first wormhole is unavoidable |
| Parallel puzzles to avoid roadblocks (Monster's Expedition, Talos 2, Lorelei) | Always another thing to try | Nine nebulae, each with its own puzzle line; finishing any 6 of 9 opens the finale |

## (c) Concrete puzzle mechanics for this project, ranked by fit

1. **Fractal maze with a depth stack (Menger sponge, Sierpinski).** Port Wolf's fractal maze into 3-D: a beacon is reachable only by zooming into sub-copy B, then C inside it, flying across, and exiting in reverse order. The HUD shows the stack of copies you are inside; wrong turns are not dead ends but endless descent, which the depth counter and the music's octave shift make legible. Teaches self-similarity and scale invariance with the existing infinite zoom and speed-scaling, and it is a mechanic no shipped game has done in 3-D.

2. **Einstein-ring alignment around the black holes.** A distant beacon's light is lensed into arcs; from exactly one orbit it closes into a ring (or N images). This is the Witness environmental puzzle and Viewfinder's "find the vantage" in a setting where the renderer already does the lensing. Expose the physics with a readout (impact parameter, image count) so solving it teaches real lensing geometry.

3. **Gravity-glide slingshot threading.** Choose an entry vector, read the predicted trajectory (Orbit / KSP), release, and thread a chain of rings whose spacing encodes Kepler's second law (closer rings must be hit faster). No fail state: a miss just re-orbits. Optional leaderboard for fewest burns, Zachtronics-style.

4. **Time-dilation reading puzzles.** A pattern on a Lichtenberg tree cycles too fast to read from far away; dive toward the horizon and your clock slows relative to it, so the pattern becomes readable (or the reverse: park deep and wait for a far clock to advance). Two synced HUD clocks make the effect the puzzle. Teaches gravitational time dilation through consequence rather than a codex paragraph.

5. **Rumor-web progression via codex cards.** Every codex card carries one fact that is also a clue ("the Kleinian limit set repeats every 3.1 ly along its axis"). An Outer Wilds-style web of connected facts is the only progression; the finale needs three facts combined (coordinates, depth, timing). Keeps the relaxing tone, needs no locks, and persists across sessions in localStorage.

6. **Resonance-pulse beam routing.** The pulse becomes a Talos emitter: fire it so that reflections off Apollonian sphere facets reach several receivers, with rules like one emitter lights every receiver it can see and two pulse colours must not cross. Receivers chime a chord from the nebula's own music when lit.

7. **Scale gates.** A passage admits only a ship of a certain size; since ship speed and scale already track local structure, the solution is to reach the gate through a region of the right iteration depth (zoom in two levels, then exit sideways). Superliminal's trick with a logic layer underneath, which is what Maquette's critics said was missing.

8. **Codex glyph decoding (Chants/Tunic).** Codex cards are partly in a glyph language whose symbols stand for the math: iterate, fold, invert, scale. Players propose meanings in a notebook; a page validates only when all glyphs on it are right. Later, a wormhole address must be written in glyphs. The vocabulary doubles as the game's science lesson.

9. **Hidden-axis slicing of the quaternion Julia set.** A dial rotates which 3-D slice of the 4-D set you see; a path exists only in one slice. Ghost-render the perpendicular slice (4D Golf) so the player can plan. Rare in games, naturally explained by the codex card.

10. **Periodic-orbit hunt in the Julia set.** Show the ship's position iterated under the Julia map as a chain of ghost markers; the goal is to park where the chain closes into a cycle (a periodic point). Teaches what an orbit is in the dynamical-systems sense; the music loops when you succeed.

11. **Ghost-mode coherence budget.** Ghost mode may phase through at most X light-years of material per charge; the puzzle is to find the thinnest wall of a sponge at the right scale. Converts a cheat into a constraint and quietly teaches that fractal surfaces are thin at every scale.

12. **Daily seeded nebula (format, not mechanic).** One seeded puzzle a day drawn from items 1 to 7, a few minutes long, with a Wordle-style spoiler-free share string (pulses used, depth reached). This is the web-native loop that built Wordle's audience and is the cheapest way to find out whether the mechanics have "the magic" before authoring a campaign.

## (d) Risks and anti-patterns players complain about

- **Mechanic over puzzle.** Viewfinder (61%) and Superliminal ("3/10 on difficulty," walking sim second half) show that a stunning trick does not survive shallow puzzles; Viewfinder specifically allowed brute force. Blow and ten Bosch: eliminate accidental solutions; Hazelden removes unintended solutions by hand.
- **Traversal tedium.** Cocoon's top complaint (65%) is carrying orbs back and forth; Maquette's is back-and-forth between scales. In a flight game every puzzle node must be reachable by gravity glide or a wormhole, never by minutes of straight flight.
- **Disorientation.** Manifold Garden needed distinct levels, windows and 2,000 playtest hours; Chants' navigation was called disorienting; Outer Wilds controls drew 20% of complaints. Self-similar fractals in 6-DoF are the worst case: add landmarks, a depth stack, breadcrumbs, and a one-key "return to last beacon."
- **Stuck with no recourse.** Outer Wilds (22% frustration, 11% guidance), The Witness ("bounce off entirely"), Tunic (stuck near the end), Baba's shelved hint system. Lorelei's untrustworthy hint phone was called "cruel." Use escalating non-spoiling hints that point to an easier deduction, plus parallel puzzle lines so there is always something else to do.
- **RNG-gated solutions.** Blue Prince's main criticism. Seeds are fine; luck-gated progress is not.
- **Bad first hour.** Parabox's early curve was "way too steep"; Islands of Insight's first five hours were "basic." Both ends fail.
- **Out-of-genre friction.** Cocoon's one-hit boss fights (46% of complaints); Portal cut every boss prototype. No fail states (Monument Valley) suits this app.
- **Hidden-rule guessing instead of deduction.** Electron Dance on Taiji: decryption plus logic yields relief, not eureka. Make the rule visible in the environment before it is required.
- **Quantity over depth.** Islands of Insight's 10,000 puzzles rated 71st percentile; critics said individual puzzles "lack depth."
- **Online dependence.** Islands' always-online lost a reviewer's progress; Sound Shapes' community levels vanished when servers closed. Keep everything client-side and shareable as seed strings.
- **Comfort and accessibility.** The Witness needed FOV patches for motion sickness; raymarched 6-DoF flight is riskier still. Audio-only clues exclude players with sound off (browsers block audio until a gesture) and deaf players; always pair sound with a visual.
- **Expectation mismatch at launch.** Tiny Kingdom's Steam build looked identical to its free demo and sold 107 reviews' worth; the Steam audience "demand[s] polish" (Roottrees). If this ever goes to Steam, the browser build must be the incubator, not the product.
