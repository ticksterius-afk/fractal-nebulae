## (a) Marble Marcher deep dive (CodeParade / HackerPoet, Jan 2019)

**What it is.** A 24-level time-trial where a glass marble rolls across a raymarched, *animating* Mandelbox-family fractal to reach a flag. Name-your-price on itch, Windows-only, 14 MB download, 30–45 min of content, GPL-2.0. itch rating 4.7/5 from 166 ratings, 189+ comments. The itch page states "no longer in active development" and redirects to the Community Edition. ([itch](https://codeparade.itch.io/marblemarcher))

**Reach.** HN front page: 345 points / 41 comments on 2019-01-02 — by far the biggest HN hit for any "fractal game" in the Algolia index ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18805717), [Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22marble%20marcher%22&tags=story)). The launch video "Marble Marcher – A Fractal Physics Game" has ≥1.75 M views / 61 K likes ([RYD cache](https://returnyoutubedislikeapi.com/votes?videoId=9U0XVdvQwAI)). GitHub: 2,600 stars, 171 forks; README: "There is currently no further major development planned for this game." ([GitHub](https://github.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher))

**How it works (from source).**
- Shader (`assets/frag.glsl`): a fold loop (`boxFold`, `mengerFold`, `sierpinskiFold`, `rotZ/rotX`, `p *= iFracScale`, `p += iFracShift`) with `FRACTAL_ITER 16`; march `MAX_MARCHES 1000`, `MIN_DIST 1e-5`, `MAX_DIST 30`; **pixel-size adaptive termination** `min_dist = max(FOVperPixel*td, MIN_DIST)`; soft shadows via a second march (`SHADOW_SHARPNESS 10`); AO from march step count; **orbit-trap colouring** `orbit = max(orbit, p.xyz*iFracCol)`; 4-sample tetrahedron normals (Quilez); glass marble with refraction 1.5 (60 % refract / 40 % reflect). ([frag.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/assets/frag.glsl))
- Physics (`src/Scene.cpp`): CPU `Scene::DE(pt)` mirrors the GPU fold loop (16 iterations); `Scene::NP(pt)` finds the **nearest surface point by folding the point forward while recording history, then unfolding backwards** — this is the "fractal physics engine" contribution. 6 sub-steps/frame; gravity `0.005 * marble_rad`; bounce 1.2; friction 0.99 ground / 0.995 air; marble "shatters" if `de < marble_rad*0.001` (crushed by animating geometry). ([Scene.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/src/Scene.cpp))
- Levels (`src/Level.cpp`): each level = scale, two angles, shift vector, colour, marble radius, start/flag positions, kill-plane, "planet mode" flag, and three **animation amplitudes** that modulate `params[1], [2], [4]` by `sin(timer*0.015)` — so the level literally breathes while you play. Examples: "Jump The Crater" (scale 1.8), "Hole In One" (scale 2.02, radius 0.009), "Around The World" (planet mode), "The Sponge" (scale 1.88). ([Level.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/src/Level.cpp))
- Cheats unlock after finishing all 24 levels (free exploration, parameter tweaking) — retained in the Community Edition. ([MMCE README](https://github.com/WAUthethird/Marble-Marcher-Community-Edition))

**What the developer said.** On HN: he says "ray-tracing" instead of "ray-marching" "for a more general audience", and that the key advantage is that the SDF *is* the acceleration structure, so the fractal can evolve in real time with no rebuild. In itch comments: "collision detection was my main contribution"; "**the distance estimator is not good enough for most fractals**, which is why I had to hand-select them"; points people at Quilez and at his Python framework **PySpace** (1.2 k stars) which generalises the nearest-point routine to other SDFs; "I'm not actively developing Marble Marcher right now." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18805717), [itch comments](https://codeparade.itch.io/marblemarcher/comments?before=151), [PySpace](https://github.com/HackerPoet/PySpace))

**Reception pattern.** Praise is almost entirely for visual/technical novelty; recurring asks are Mac support, 4K, left-hand controls, a jump, mid-air control, and *more/procedural levels*. Performance reports range from 2 FPS (2008 laptop) to 120+ FPS at 1600×900. ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18805717), [itch comments](https://codeparade.itch.io/marblemarcher/comments?before=151))

**Community Edition (MMCE).** Created because 1.1.0 was the last feature update. 289 stars / 24 forks. Adds: compute-shader engine with hot-editable **shader packs** (PBR+TXAA, path-traced GI, "original", "simple", "neon"), level/fractal editor, 30 built-in levels + MegaLevelPack, gamepad/remap, **recording/replay "for speedrun verification"**, 10 K screenshots, i18n, and "100 %–200 % faster" than the original. Latest release 1.4.6 ("minor bugfixes"); the 1.4.x line went beta5 (gamepad) → 1.4.3 "Real Time Path Tracing". MMCE itch: 4.8/5 from 13 ratings, 51 MB. ([MMCE GitHub](https://github.com/WAUthethird/Marble-Marcher-Community-Edition), [releases](https://github.com/WAUthethird/Marble-Marcher-Community-Edition/releases), [MMCE itch](https://michaelmoroz.itch.io/mmce))

**Speedrunning.** speedrun.com game `46w3l991`, categories: Any% (all official levels through "Planet Crusher", in-game timer), 100 % (through "Fatal Fissures"), and per-level ILs. Any% board has 20 runs; WR 9:30.05 (Oct 2019); runs span 2019-01-19 → 2024-04-26. The first 100 verified runs were all submitted within three days of 2019-01-19 by 24 players — i.e., a launch spike then a long tail. A Discord serves "speedrunning and development". ([categories](https://www.speedrun.com/api/v1/games/46w3l991/categories), [Any% board](https://www.speedrun.com/api/v1/leaderboards/46w3l991/category/9d8xv9q2), [runs](https://www.speedrun.com/api/v1/runs?game=46w3l991&max=200&status=verified), [links](https://itch.io/t/672714/links-and-resources))

**Browser ports (directly relevant to us).** (1) "Marble Marcher in a browser" by Antz: WebGL, Chromium-tested, default 1280×720, "desktop GPUs only; laptops will struggle", Game Jolt leaderboards. (2) codefiddler's **WebGPU** port (Show HN Jan 2025, Chrome-only, GPL-3). (3) A Shadertoy edition (`3lKyDR`). Takeaway: the exact Marble Marcher workload runs at 60 fps in-browser on a dedicated GPU at ~720p. ([Antz port](https://antzgames.itch.io/marble-marcher-in-a-browser), [codefiddler port](https://codefiddler.itch.io/marble-marcher))

**Lessons.** (i) The animating-parameter level is the real design invention: the same formula yields 24 distinct levels and the "crushed by geometry" tension. (ii) 30–45 min of content was enough for virality but not for retention; the community's first act was a *level editor*. (iii) Exact CPU-mirrored collision is what made it feel like a real game rather than a demo. (iv) Hand-pick fractals/parameter regions where the DE is well-behaved — the author says most aren't.

## (b) Claybook and other SDF/physics games

**Claybook (Second Order, 2018).** A whole world made of clay, simulated and rendered as SDFs on the GPU, integrated into UE4 at 60 fps on consoles. GDC 2018 tutorial by Sebastian Aaltonen: "GPU-based clay and fluid simulator, fully deformable world and characters, signed distance field modelling, and ray traced visuals." ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025316/Advanced-Graphics-Techniques-Tutorial-GPU), [slides via GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/gdc-2018-presentations/)) Aaltonen later: SDFs "are super nice for physics sim… you always know where is the closest surface, even inside the objects. Pushing particles out is simple. Triangles = thin surface. Tunneling is a real issue." ([tweet, via search snippet](https://x.com/SebAaltonen/status/2039248586974613812))

Mechanics: morph between sphere/cube/cylinder/wheel (+ duck, rocket, magnet, orb); hold a button to **absorb clay** and carve paths; movement leaves tracks; "water" is thick clay ("rice pudding"); 20 levels in 5 "books" with destroy-X / fill-container / time-trial goals, 3-star grading, 4-player couch co-op, rewind, and a late-added level editor. Switch port renders the volume at half resolution and "looks quite similar due to the nature of the sign distance field." ([GamePitt 8.5/10](https://www.gamepitt.co.uk/claybook-ps4-review/), [oprainfall interview](https://operationrainfall.com/2019/04/16/interview-second-orders-sami-saarinen-talks-claybook/))

Reception: Steam **Mixed, 61 % of 186 reviews**; Nintendojo 77 % ("overly brief; challenge too low"); MSPoweruser: "pretty to look at but lacking in substance." The studio admitted the editor landed late, targeted experts, and "community response was lower than hoped." ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/661920/Claybook/), [Nintendojo](https://www.nintendojo.com/reviews/review-claybook), [MSPU](https://mspoweruser.com/review-claybook-is-pretty-to-look-at-but-lacking-in-substance/)) **Lesson: world-class SDF tech with thin goals sells poorly; the tech must serve a loop with escalating objectives.**

**Dreams (Media Molecule).** Alex Evans' SIGGRAPH 2015 talk: scenes are CSG trees "evaluated on-the-fly to high resolution signed distance fields, from which we generate dense multi-resolution point clouds" — i.e., they used SDFs for *authoring* and abandoned pure per-pixel raymarching for *rendering*. Epic's Daniel Wright (same course) uses distance-field ray tracing for occlusion in UE4. ([Advances 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/index.html), [MM blog](https://www.mediamolecule.com/blog/article/siggraph_2015))

**Yedoma Globula (Bananaft / Gregory Ivanov).** The nearest thing to a shipped raymarched fractal *open world*: first-person sandbox over hand-picked procedurally generated fractal terrain, rendered with "raymarching via SDFs and hierarchical cone-marching," while **entities use traditional polygons**. Jetpack, climbing, flying boats, co-op servers. $10 on itch, 4.7/5 from 118 ratings, 66+ comments, GTX 960 for 1080p60. Dev's own words: "currently there is not much in terms of actual gameplay"; last update April 2022; not on Steam. ([itch](https://bananaft.itch.io/yedomaglobula)) Lesson: a hybrid renderer (raymarched world + rasterised actors) is the proven pattern, and "exploration only" plateaus.

## (d) Zoom / scale as a mechanic — findings

- **Deep-zoom video is a mass audience.** Maths Town's "Eye of the Universe" (depth e1091, rendered >1 week in Kalles Fraktaler, aired on *The Big Bang Theory*) sits at ≥3.9 M views; "Deepest Mandelbrot Set Zoom Animation ever… 10^275" at ≥12.1 M views ([RYD 1](https://returnyoutubedislikeapi.com/votes?videoId=pCpLWbHVNhk), [RYD 2](https://returnyoutubedislikeapi.com/votes?videoId=0jGaio87u3A), [archive](https://archive.org/details/youtube-pCpLWbHVNhk), [Maths Town](https://www.maths.town/videos/)). The creator explicitly frames it as ambient: "long enough to play your favourite album in the background."
- **Why it works:** (1) the *reveal* — a mini-brot emerging from chaos is a payoff with built-in anticipation; (2) scale vertigo; (3) music pairing; (4) record-chasing ("deepest ever", "a new record!"). Tools: Kalles Fraktaler (perturbation + series approximation, "100× faster than commercial programs") and XaoS (continuous real-time zoom, now in-browser) ([KF](https://mathr.co.uk/kf/kf.html), [XaoS](https://xaos-project.github.io/), [deep-zoom theory, HN 183 pts](https://mathr.co.uk/blog/2021-05-14_deep_zoom_theory_and_practice.html)). Zoomquilt (2004) "immediately went viral" and spawned sequels ([Zoomquilt](https://zoomquilt.org/)); Scale of the Universe 2 is a slider through 60 orders of magnitude ([htwins](https://htwins.net/scale2/)).
- **How games give zoom a goal:**
  - *Nested destination*: Fractal Block World's rescue quest lives several scale levels down; reviewers: "This entire enormous space is just the bulb on a small flower"; "If I see a dungeon inside a speck of air on a leaf… I investigate further." ([reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1540570/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated))
  - *Threshold unlocks*: Katamari's size gates (thumbtacks → clouds) turn growth into progression ([Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy)).
  - *Paradox puzzles*: Parabox's self-containing boxes; Maquette's table↔world.
  - *Staged scale*: Spore sold 2 M in three weeks but critics called the stages "too fleeting" — scale transitions need depth at each scale, not just a transition ([Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore_(2008_video_game))).
  - *Catalogue/discovery*: Noctis GUIDE, Everything's "being" — low-pressure goals suit relaxing products.
  - *Time pressure on a breathing world*: Marble Marcher's sin-modulated parameters.

## (e) Technical feasibility — WebGL2 raymarch, GTX 1080-class, 60 fps

Baselines: Marble Marcher (16 fold iterations, up to 1000 steps, soft shadow + AO + refractive marble) runs at 60 fps in-browser at ~720p on a dedicated GPU ([Antz port](https://antzgames.itch.io/marble-marcher-in-a-browser)); Yedoma Globula targets 1080p60 on a GTX 960 using cone-marching plus rasterised actors ([itch](https://bananaft.itch.io/yedomaglobula)). Fractal Nebulae already mirrors DEs on the CPU, which is exactly the Marble Marcher precondition.

| Gameplay feature | Feasible? | How | Cost | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multiple agents/objects *inside* the fractal | Yes | Hybrid: raymarch the fractal, rasterise meshes with three.js, composite by depth (write `gl_FragDepth` from march distance). Avoid putting many objects in the SDF union — branches are "very expensive… on the web with WebGL." | ~free per object beyond raster cost | [Quilez bounding](https://iquilezles.org/articles/sdfbounding/), Yedoma |
| Physics for small bodies (sphere collision) | Yes (shipped) | Sphere tracing as collision: `DE(p) < r` ⇒ push out along gradient (4-eval tetrahedron normal) or Marble Marcher's exact `NP()` for fold-type fractals (Mandelbox/Menger/Sierpinski/KIFS). Sub-step 4–6×/frame. | CPU: ~N bodies × 6 substeps × (1 DE + 4 DE for normal) | [Scene.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/src/Scene.cpp), [normals](https://iquilezles.org/articles/normalsSDF/), Aaltonen |
| Orbit traps as "resources"/materials | Yes, free | The march already accumulates trap values; read trap at the hit point (and at the target-lock point via CPU DE) and bucket into element types. | 0 extra GPU; CPU: one DE eval | [MM frag.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/assets/frag.glsl), [Quilez traps](https://iquilezles.org/articles/ftrapsgeometric/) |
| Procedural placement of items on the surface | Yes | Random seed point → iterate `p -= DE(p)·∇DE(p)` a few times (gradient descent to the surface), reject if DE bound misbehaves; or NP() for fold fractals. Run in a worker. | CPU ms-scale per 100 items | [Quilez normals](https://iquilezles.org/articles/normalsSDF/), [PySpace](https://github.com/HackerPoet/PySpace) |
| Path-finding through interiors | Yes, coarse | Sample DE on a 32–64³ grid around the player (free if DE > clearance), A* on it, then sphere-trace line-of-sight to smooth. Re-sample when parameters animate. | 64³ = 262 k DE evals ≈ tens of ms in a worker | Marble Marcher DE mirror; Claybook ("you always know the closest surface, even inside") |
| Destructible/mutable fractals via parameters | Yes (MM does it) | Animate scale/angles/shift uniforms; CPU mirror keeps collision exact. Marble Marcher's "crushed" rule is the enemy. | 0 extra | [Level.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/MarbleMarcher/master/src/Level.cpp) |
| Local carving (Claybook-style holes) | Limited | Union of N subtractive spheres in the SDF (cap ~16–32 with a bounding test), or bake an SDF into a 3D texture rendered slice-by-slice via FBO (no compute in WebGL2). | Each extra primitive costs every march step | [Quilez distfunctions (old path 404; see raymarchingdf)](https://iquilezles.org/articles/raymarchingdf/), Claybook GDC |
| "Painting" the fractal | Yes | (a) 3D RGBA8 mask texture (128³ = 8 MB) sampled at hit point; (b) **paint by orbit-trap bucket** — zero memory, and painting one copy paints every self-similar sibling (a feature). | (a) one texture fetch at hit; (b) free | MM orbit colouring |
| Light/shadow puzzles | Yes | Quilez soft shadows: one extra march per shaded pixel; Aaltonen's improvement removes banding. Movable light = uniform. | ~+30–60 % march cost | [rmshadows](https://iquilezles.org/articles/rmshadows/), [2D intuition](https://www.rykap.com/2020/09/23/distance-fields/) |
| Portals | Yes | When the ray crosses a portal disc, transform ray origin/dir and continue marching (one extra march segment); CodeParade's engine does portals by rasterised stencils, but in a raymarcher it's a ray transform. Wormholes in the app already do a variant. | +1 march for portal pixels | CodeParade engine video |
| Domain repetition for "infinite" fields | Yes, with care | Correct repetition of varying instances costs 8× in 3D; mirrored repetition keeps 1×. | 1×–8× | [sdfrepetition](https://iquilezles.org/articles/sdfrepetition/) |
| Deep-zoom (Mandelbrot-style) in 3D | Partial | float32 limits ~1e-6 relative; beyond that, re-centre the coordinate frame on the camera (what the app's collision depth implies). True perturbation theory is 2D-escape-time specific. | — | [mathr](https://mathr.co.uk/blog/2021-05-14_deep_zoom_theory_and_practice.html), [Quilez DE](https://iquilezles.org/articles/distancefractals/) |

Caveat from CodeParade: DEs for many formulas are unreliable; hand-select parameter regions and validate `DE(p) ≤ true distance` empirically before gameplay depends on it.

## (f) Fractal-native mechanic ideas, ranked

1. **Breathing levels (parameter time-trials).** Copy Marble Marcher's proven core: a nebula whose scale/angle/shift oscillates on a timer; the player must thread a route before a passage closes. Exact collision already exists; add the "crushed" fail state. Lowest risk, strongest proof.
2. **Self-similar painting.** Resonance pulse paints by orbit-trap bucket; painting one feature paints all its fractal siblings. Goal: "paint the whole nebula with ≤N pulses" — a puzzle about reading self-similarity. Zero GPU memory.
3. **Element prospecting via orbit traps.** Trap values become mineral classes shown on the codex card; quests ask for a rare trap combination that only exists in deep detail. Turns "zoom into infinite detail" into a goal (Fractal Block World's "dungeon in a speck on a leaf").
4. **Scale-ladder rescue/beacons.** Place beacons several decades of scale apart (gradient-descent placement); each beacon is only visible from the next scale down. Gives the deep-zoom reveal a destination (FBW's rescue quest, Katamari thresholds).
5. **Shadow-key doors.** Movable light source + soft-shadow march; a door opens only when the fractal's shadow of a specific lobe covers a sensor. Cheap, visually unique to raymarching.
6. **Gravity-glide courier in a Menger interior.** Grid-sampled DE path-finding gives an NPC/ghost racer; player must beat it through the sponge's interior. Hybrid raster agents keep cost flat.
7. **Portal stitching across nebulae.** Wormholes already exist; make them player-placed (ray-transform portals) with the puzzle of lining up two self-similar regions so a continuous flight path exists.
8. **Parameter cheats as a second game.** Marble Marcher unlocks parameter cheats after completion and MMCE's editor was the community's first ask: a "nebula editor" with shareable seeds (URL-encoded) extends the 30–45 minute cliff.
9. **Time-dilation speedruns.** Combine the existing black-hole time dilation with in-game-timer categories (Any% / per-nebula ILs). Speedrunning attached itself to Marble Marcher within 3 days; replay recording "for verification" was an MMCE feature.
10. **Catalogue the infinite (Noctis GUIDE).** Players name and submit discovered regions (parameters + camera pose); a shared codex grows. Suits the relaxing/educational brief and costs nothing in rendering.
11. **Kleinian limit-set lighthouse.** Light-only puzzles where the player positions a light so a Kleinian/Apollonian structure casts a target silhouette — pure soft-shadow play.
12. **Crush-escape in planet mode.** MM's "planet mode" levels (gravity toward centre) plus animating scale: the planet grows around you; find the exit before pockets close.
